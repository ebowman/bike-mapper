# Feasibility summary: a flow-optimising road-cycling planner on open data

This note only synthesises the other notes in this directory; no new research. Every claim links to the note section it comes from. Numbers are quoted as the notes state them. Where a source is a bead note or a file under `data/samples/` rather than a research note, it is said so.

## TL;DR

**Go, with caveats.** Open data can price the dominant cause of stops (traffic signals, 71% of stops at units with at least 5 passes) well enough to build a probability-weighted "flow" profile for BRouter, and one hands-on route moved as intended (Dublin fringe route: expected stops/km down 23% for +0.65 km, see [routing-engines](routing-engines.md#hands-on-check)). Elevation (Copernicus GLO-30) and a climb layer derived from OSM + GLO-30 are feasible, and OSM is a usable routing graph. The single biggest risk is that the stop-probability weights are calibrated on **one rider's data (81 usable rides, 63 snapped to the extracts, 2,618 km, Ireland + Berlin) with two imperfect stop instruments**: the same signal gets p(stop) 0.10 in Ireland and 0.19 in Berlin depending on how a "stop" was detected, the Irish part alone is thin, and there is no public substitute for ride data ([stop-model-validation](stop-model-validation.md#verdict), [empirical-stops](empirical-stops.md#verdict)). Validation used Eric's own Garmin export, so this is not a public-traces-only result; public OSM traces were tested and are not usable (same empirical-stops verdict). The explained-fraction headline (88.5%) is an upper-end figure; its lower bound is 62.7%. Map of the study outputs (expected stops/km, observed stop units, flow vs fastbike routes, unexplained units): [docs/map/index.html](../map/index.html), built with `make map`, see [README](README.md#map-viewer).

## The flow answer

Flow, in Eric's definition, is stretches where a bike rarely has to stop. **Question: can open data predict per-segment stop frequency?** Answer: **partly, and well enough to rank routes and to price signals; not well enough to predict stops at unsigned junctions, and not independently of Eric's own ride data.** Evidence on the map: [docs/map/index.html](../map/index.html) (ways coloured by expected stops/km, observed stop units, flow vs baseline routes, unexplained units).

### What is obtainable, and at what accuracy

- **OSM stop-cause features are largely there.** About 90% of Dublin City Council signal sites (850 SCATS sites) have a `highway=traffic_signals` node within 30 m (90.2%; 91.4% with `crossing=traffic_signals`; 94.9% for junction-type sites, 85.8% for pedestrian-type sites); this is Dublin, the best-mapped area, so an upper bound for rural areas, and it measures recall only, not whether OSM signals still exist ([osm-stop-features, SCATS recall](osm-stop-features.md#dublin-scats-completeness-osm-recall)). Signals, stop and give-way nodes are mostly direction-qualified, and an explicit sign on an approach node suppresses the inferred yield at its junction for 95.6% of approach events in Ireland ([approach attribution](osm-stop-features.md#approach-node-attribution-of-explicit-signalstopgive_way-nodes)); the Berlin figure is 92.7%, from `data/samples/osm_stop_features_berlin.md`, not from a research note. The note's own verdict is "Usable with caveats" ([verdict](osm-stop-features.md#verdict)).
- **Observed-stop data exists only as Eric's own rides.** 86 outdoor rides / 3,720 km pulled from Garmin Connect as 1 s FIT files, 81 usable after cleaning; 63 rides / 2,618 km snap to the Ireland and Berlin extracts (31 rides / 1,128 km in Ireland, 32 rides / 1,490 km in Berlin) ([ride-data-acquisition](ride-data-acquisition.md#verdict), [empirical-stops](empirical-stops.md#verdict), [Berlin extension](empirical-stops.md#berlin-extension-dhy15)). Public OSM GPS traces: 56 cycling-pace traces / 316 km in the test bbox and 18 nodes with at least 5 passes, so no public substitute ([empirical-stops](empirical-stops.md#verdict)).
- **Per-class stop probability, combined Ireland + Berlin** (leader clustering r = 25 m, units with at least 5 passes; range across four groupings in brackets): signals 0.158 (0.134-0.253), give_way 0.041 (0.037-0.067), stop sign 0.021 (0.021-0.044), inferred yield 0.016 (0.014-0.018), equal-class uncontrolled 0.021 (0.021-0.041), mid-way 0.003. Signals carry the weight: 262 of 367 stops (71%) at units with at least 5 passes are at signal-class units ([stop-model-validation verdict](stop-model-validation.md#verdict)). Roundabout entries (0 stops in 22 passes) and level crossings (23 passes) are under-powered, so they should not be tuned from this data (same verdict).
- **Explained fraction 88.5% is an upper-end figure; it also counts unit passes as the maximum over member nodes rather than recounting from the ride tracks, which flatters p(stop) at busy junctions ([stop-model-validation](stop-model-validation.md#verdict)).** Proximity classification (OSM cause within 20 m) explains 88.5% of observed stops (Ireland 80.5%, Berlin 94.1%); classifying by member nodes only gives 62.7% (Ireland 64.9%, Berlin 61.1%) ([validation, explained fraction](stop-model-validation.md#hands-on-check)). The naive rule "cause present means stop" has precision 5% (5.2% combined) at 100% recall, because 629 of 1,629 units are predicted positive and only 33 have p of at least 0.5 (same section). Per the dhy.11 review recorded in the bead notes (not in the research note), predicted-positive units carry 38% of passes but 92% of stops, which is what makes a probability-weighted cost useful even though the binary rule is not.
- **Recommended weights for BRouter** (relative node cost, signals = 300; headline, range over the four groupings; current `flow.brf` in the last column): signals 300 (anchor; current 300); signalled crossing: treat as signals, 300 (up to 390; the current 150 "is not supported"); give_way 72 (68-84; Ireland-only 152-217 vs Berlin-only 17-23; current 80, keep about 80); stop sign 49 (49-79; lower to about 60; current 150); inferred yield 29 (26-33; needs preprocessing, BRouter cannot infer); equal-class 8 (8-35; at most about 10-35); roundabout entry and level crossing: under-powered, leave unchanged / out ([validation verdict](stop-model-validation.md#verdict)). Weights are on a 300 scale of net stop-seconds; whether `flow.brf` expresses node cost in the same unit as per-metre cost "is not verified here" (same verdict).
- **A router can act on it.** BRouter node `initialcost` handles signals, stop, give_way and mini-roundabouts directly; roundabout entry works through the way-context classifier workaround; turn cost is symmetric in angle, so BRouter cannot penalise right turns specifically ([routing-engines, hands-on](routing-engines.md#hands-on-check)). With untuned weights 300/150/80, the Dublin fringe route (Rathfarnham to Stepaside) went from 2.98 to 2.29 expected stops/km (-23%) for +3.5% distance (+0.65 km, +5 m ascent); the Wicklow route did not change because it passes essentially no stop-cause nodes (0.21 stops/km) (same section). Injecting measured per-node probabilities requires bucketing a `stop_prob` tag, a custom `lookups.dat` and a local rd5 rebuild, and is **not tested** ([integration path](routing-engines.md#integration-path-for-dhy11-per-node-stop-probability)).

### What is missing

- **Unsigned-junction priority.** Only about 60k explicit stop/give-way nodes against 649k junction nodes (489k excluding plain way splits), so most yields are inferred from road class ([osm-stop-features verdict](osm-stop-features.md#verdict)). Measured p(stop) at inferred yields and equal-class junctions is low (0.016 and 0.021), so the inference mostly adds noise, not signal ([stop-model-validation](stop-model-validation.md#verdict)).
- **Instrument dependence.** Ireland rides have auto-pause off (stop = slow-speed run), Berlin rides have it on (stop = timer gap); signal p(stop) is 0.10 vs 0.19, and the give_way weight is 152-217 in Ireland vs 17-23 in Berlin ([cross-region caveat](stop-model-validation.md#hands-on-check)). Weights are therefore instrument-dependent, not a property of the road.
- **Signalled crossings are inseparable from signals** under proximity classification (the class is absorbed); "treat as signals" rests on the member-node view (0.27 vs 0.18, weight 390) ([verdict table](stop-model-validation.md#verdict)).
- **One rider.** Single-class confidence intervals are wide (give_way 0.027-0.062), and about 40% of the top unexplained units are viewpoint/cafe stops that no router model can predict (same verdict).
- **No public substitute for ride data** ([empirical-stops](empirical-stops.md#verdict)); Strava API data of other users cannot be used under the API Agreement (same verdict, [sources](empirical-stops.md#sources-evaluated)).
- **Signal timing, queueing and traffic-dependent stops** are absent from OSM ([osm-stop-features verdict](osm-stop-features.md#verdict)); AADT would not materially sharpen the stop model on current evidence ([motor-traffic-flow](motor-traffic-flow.md#link-to-the-flow-finding-dhy11)).

### Where the notes disagree or hedge

- **Irish data is marginal, yet the validation uses it.** [empirical-stops](empirical-stops.md#verdict) says Eric's Irish history is "only marginally" big enough (31 rides / 1,128 km; 90 nodes with at least 5 passes and at least 1 stop; too thin for rare causes), and brings in Berlin to double the node set. [stop-model-validation](stop-model-validation.md#verdict) then pools both regions, although the two regions use different stop instruments. The pooled table is therefore a blend, not a calibrated Irish model. Ride counts also differ by stage: 86 pulled / 3,720 km ([ride-data-acquisition](ride-data-acquisition.md#verdict)), 81 usable / 3,530 km, 63 snapped / 2,618 km ([empirical-stops](empirical-stops.md#verdict)).
- **Expected stops/km in osm-stop-features is not a probability.** It counts each yield/give-way as 1 stop and the equal-class term as 0.5, and says inferred yields should not be treated as ground truth until validated ([osm-stop-features verdict](osm-stop-features.md#verdict)); validation then measures p of about 0.016-0.021 for those classes. So the -23% result in routing-engines and the stops/km colouring on the map use the heuristic scale, and are rankings, not forecasts. routing-engines itself calls the 300/150/80 weights "untuned guesses" ([hands-on](routing-engines.md#hands-on-check)), and validation finds the untuned ordering (stop 150 above give_way 80) inverted relative to Eric's behaviour ([verdict](stop-model-validation.md#verdict)). The route experiment has not been rerun with the validated weights.
- **Signal recall is Dublin-only and an upper bound** for rural areas ([osm-stop-features](osm-stop-features.md#dublin-scats-completeness-osm-recall)); the precision of OSM signals (nodes that no longer exist) is not measured.
- **Dublin-only evaluation of the route effect.** The Wicklow pair showed no change because there is nothing to avoid ([routing-engines](routing-engines.md#hands-on-check)); the benefit exists only in built-up areas, where the unpriced right-turn component is of the same order as the stop component (8 right turns vs 44 expected stops).

## Recommended stack

- **DEM:** Copernicus GLO-30 (`copernicus-dem-30m` on AWS, no login, COG). On Wicklow Gap it reproduced the published ascent within 0.9% ([elevation](elevation.md#verdict)). Compute gradients over at least 100 m windows after smoothing. One tile (N53 W007) is 26,537,243 bytes (26.5 MB) ([elevation, hands-on](elevation.md#hands-on-check)).
- **Routing engine:** BRouter (MIT; node-cost DSL; elevation built in; ran with sub-second routes). **Runner-up: GraphHopper** (Apache-2.0, per-request custom model; needs Java for any node-level cost). OSRM expresses signed turn angles best but has no elevation; Valhalla exposes no stop-cost knob for bicycle ([routing-engines](routing-engines.md#verdict)).
- **Pipeline sketch** (the injection and rd5 steps are per [routing-engines, integration path](routing-engines.md#integration-path-for-dhy11-per-node-stop-probability), **not tested**):
  1. Geofabrik PBF (ODbL) ([osm-road-network](osm-road-network.md#sources-evaluated)).
  2. `scripts/osm_stop_features.py`: classify stop-cause features and infer yields per way and direction ([osm-stop-features](osm-stop-features.md#yield-inference-rule-per-way-per-direction-of-travel-at-each-node)).
  3. Map classes to the validated relative weights (see the flow answer), as `stop_prob` buckets 0-10 on nodes via pyosmium, and add the `stop_prob` values to a copy of `lookups.dat`.
  4. Rebuild the rd5 tiles locally with `mapcreator` (needed; the pre-built brouter.de tiles cannot carry the new tag).
  5. A `flow.brf` profile whose node `initialcost` reads `stop_prob`; run BRouter; GPX out for the flow profile.
  6. Climb layer from the derivation spec on PBF + GLO-30 ([climbs](climbs.md#derivation-approach)), matched along routes with the step-10 rule.
- **Hosting shape:** the notes measured a single BRouter JVM serving pre-built Irish tiles (RSS 165 MB at start, 270 MB after 7.5 minutes and 8 routes; 0.2-0.6 s per route). They did not test any hosted deployment, so hosting beyond a single-process server is not covered by the notes ([routing-engines, Sources evaluated](routing-engines.md#sources-evaluated)).

## What ClimbFinder has that open data cannot give

Honest gaps, per [climbs](climbs.md#verdict):

- **A curated list with provenance.** ClimbFinder lists 239 climbs for Ireland; OSM and Wikidata have only 23 + 17 pass/saddle nodes and 22 passes, so pass nodes alone could name about a tenth of them; the naming fallbacks in the spec are required. No open dataset of climbs exists.
- **The climb points formula.** ClimbFinder's per-section formula is not published; the spec's sum of g^2/10 over 100 m sections "fits one data point". Three other Irish climbs are consistent only on assumed numbers, so the point scale needs calibrating on more climbs before categories are trusted.
- **Community data and polish.** Not assessed by any note: the notes compare only data, licences and derivable metrics, so ClimbFinder's community features, ratings, UI and app polish are acknowledged as gaps but unsourced here. The only sourced statement is that open data supplies "only names and points" ([climbs, verdict](climbs.md#verdict)).
- **Trunk and unpaved climbs.** The spec excludes `highway=trunk` (N-roads) and tracks; several ClimbFinder Irish climbs run on N-roads, so recall may suffer ([climbs, open questions](climbs.md#open-questions-for-eric)).
- **What open data does give:** on Wicklow Gap from Laragh the spec reproduces ClimbFinder to within 1.5% on gain, 3% on length and 1% on difficulty points (332.1 m, 6.58 km and 204.0 points vs 337 m, 6.8 km and 202 points), same category (3) and same name ("Wicklow Gap from Glendalough") ([derivation approach, dry run](climbs.md#derivation-approach)). One climb only; ClimbFinder pages are for evaluation, not shipping.
