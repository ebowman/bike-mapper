# Elevation: open DEM comparison and profile accuracy on Wicklow Gap

## Question

Which open elevation model is good enough for ClimbFinder-grade gradient profiles (per-100 m gradient, climb categorisation) in Ireland, and how is it accessed? Measured on one known Irish climb, Wicklow Gap (R756) from Laragh, against ClimbFinder's published numbers (bead dhy.3). Feeds the climbs derivation spec (dhy.4) and the synthesis (dhy.9).

## Hands-on check

Climb: Wicklow Gap from Laragh, OSM `ref=R756`. `scripts/build_climb_gpx.py` stitches four OSM ways (ids 4629662 reversed, 40395921, 40447313, 128947912) from an Overpass `out geom` query into `data/samples/wicklow_gap_r756.gpx` (252 points, 7,737 m polyline; the profile's last 25 m sample is at 7,725 m. Starts at the Laragh junction 53.0090 N 6.3077 W and ends at 53.0418 N 6.3966 W, the end of way 128947912, which is about 90 m before the road crest: the last step is still +4.5% at 473.6 m and ClimbFinder's end samples 475.6 m, so about 2 m of ascent is missed. The brief's summit 53.033 N was approximate). The public Overpass servers were busy for several minutes; the third mirror answered (a retry loop is the lesson).

Downloaded (all under gitignored `data/dem/`, 2026-10-20):
- `data/dem/glo30_N53_W007.tif`, Copernicus GLO-30, 26,537,243 bytes (26.5 MB), float32, 2400x3600.
- `data/dem/srtm/N53W007.hgt` (from `N53W007.hgt.gz`, 4,918,819 bytes gz; 25,934,402 bytes unpacked), skadi SRTM-derived 1 arcsec, int16, 3601x3601, nodata -32768.
- `data/dem/r756_overpass.json`, 31,554 bytes.
Not downloaded: EU-DEM (EU Login), GSI/OPW LiDAR (no coverage, portal), EA LiDAR (England only), Tailte (no open product found), NASA original SRTM (Earthdata login).

Reproduce:
```
uv run python scripts/build_climb_gpx.py --json data/dem/r756_overpass.json --ways=-4629662,40395921,40447313,128947912 --out data/samples/wicklow_gap_r756.gpx --name "Wicklow Gap from Laragh (R756, OSM)"
uv run python scripts/elevation_profile.py --gpx data/samples/wicklow_gap_r756.gpx --dem data/dem/glo30_N53_W007.tif --label glo30
uv run python scripts/elevation_profile.py --gpx data/samples/wicklow_gap_r756.gpx --dem data/dem/srtm/N53W007.hgt --label srtm1_skadi
# match the reference start point: last 6.8 km only
uv run python scripts/elevation_profile.py --gpx data/samples/wicklow_gap_r756.gpx --dem data/dem/glo30_N53_W007.tif --label glo30 --tail-m 6800
uv run python scripts/elevation_profile.py --gpx data/samples/wicklow_gap_r756.gpx --dem data/dem/srtm/N53W007.hgt --label srtm1_skadi --tail-m 6800
```
`--dem` also accepts the https URL of the COG (read via `/vsicurl/`; gives identical numbers). Samples every 25 m along the WGS84 geodesic, manual bilinear from the 4 surrounding cell centres, ascent both as raw sum of positive 25 m steps and with 3 m hysteresis, max gradient over sliding 100 m windows. Samples with no-data/void neighbours are logged to stderr and skipped; none were hit (0 masked or void cells in either full tile, 0 skipped samples).

Reference: ClimbFinder, "Wicklow Gap from Glendalough", https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/wicklow-gap-glendalough (fetched 2026-10-20): length 6.8 km, total ascent 337 m, average gradient 4.9%, steepest 100 m 10.7%. Its start is a few hundred metres further up than my Laragh junction (mine starts 151 m high and is 7.7 km; hence the tail variant, which starts at 140 m and 6.78 km). Wikipedia gives the pass height as 475 m (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicklow_Gap). ClimbFinder does not publish its DEM or smoothing, so it is a comparison reference, not ground truth.

Results (CSV in `data/samples/wicklow_gap_profile_<label>.csv`):

| DEM | track | length m | summit m | ascent (3 m hyst) m | ascent raw m | avg gradient % | max 100 m gradient % |
|-----|-------|---------:|---------:|--------------------:|-------------:|---------------:|---------------------:|
| ClimbFinder reference | 6.8 km | 6800 | (475 per Wikipedia) | 337 | not published | 4.9 | 10.7 |
| GLO-30 | tail 6.8 km | 6775 | 473.6 | 334 (-3, -0.9%) | 341 | 4.92 | 11.5 (+0.8) |
| SRTM 1" (skadi) | tail 6.8 km | 6775 | 476.6 | 341 (+4, +1.2%) | 355 | 4.96 | 12.6 (+1.9) |
| GLO-30 | full Laragh 7.7 km | 7725 | 473.6 | 344 | 352 | 4.17 | 11.5 |
| SRTM 1" (skadi) | full Laragh 7.7 km | 7725 | 476.6 | 347 | 364 | 4.21 | 12.6 |

SRTM minus GLO-30 along the full profile: mean +2.1 m, RMS 3.4 m, max 10.5 m. Absolute heights differ in part by geoid: GLO-30 is referenced to EGM2008 and SRTM to EGM96; over Ireland the two geoids differ by decimetres, which does not matter for gradients (not measured here). The summit of both lands within 2 m of 475 m. Noise is visible in the raw ascent sums (raw minus hysteresis: 7 m for GLO-30, 14 m for SRTM at 25 m spacing), so a hysteresis or smoothing step is needed to reach ClimbFinder-like totals. Max gradient is the fragile statistic: the two DEMs put the steepest 100 m in different places (GLO-30 at 3.0 km, SRTM at 0.5 km from the tail start), and the SRTM one is 1.9 points over the reference. Limits of this check: n = 1 climb, open moorland with almost no tree cover (GLO-30 is a surface model, so tree-lined roads will read high, untested), reference with unknown provenance.

Hosted services were not called; their limits come from the docs listed in the Sources table.

## Verdict

Usable with caveats. Recommended DEM for Ireland: Copernicus GLO-30 (`copernicus-dem-30m` on AWS, no login, COG so range-readable): on Wicklow Gap it reproduced the published 337 m ascent to -3 m (-0.9%) and average gradient exactly (4.9%), with max 100 m gradient +0.8 points high (11.5% vs 10.7%). Fallback for the rest of Europe: GLO-30 as well, since it is global and uniformly licensed. Where better data exists the 1 m LiDAR products (England EA, OGL v3, no login) are preferable, and for Ireland nothing open and scriptable beat GLO-30: Tailte has no open terrain product I could find, and GSI/OPW LiDAR is patchy (no coverage at Wicklow Gap) with only portal downloads. EU-DEM v1.1 is superseded, needs a login and has a published +-7 m RMSE, so it is not worth the trouble; SRTM via the skadi mirror is a workable no-login second source (+1.2% ascent, +1.9 points max gradient) but noisier. The planner should compute gradients over >= 100 m windows after smoothing/hysteresis and never trust single-sample or 25 m gradients from a 30 m DEM.
