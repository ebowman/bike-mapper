# Climbs: open climb datasets and a derivation spec from OSM + DEM

## Question

Does any openly licensed dataset of named road-cycling climbs exist, and if not, what does it take to derive one from OSM + a DEM so a ClimbFinder-style "climbs along / near your route" feature is possible? Depends on the OSM road-network note (dhy.2, road classes) and the elevation note (`elevation.md`, dhy.3: GLO-30 profile code and data). Checked 2026-10-20.

## Hands-on check

Findings from the survey:
- No openly licensed dataset of named, gradient-profiled road-cycling climbs exists. Every curated climb database (ClimbFinder, Quaeldich, climbbybike, cols-cyclisme, Cycling Cols) is proprietary or unlicensed, and Strava segments are barred by the API Agreement. The open sources (Wikidata CC0, OSM ODbL) hold only points: 22 Wikidata passes (about 21 in the Republic) and 23 OSM pass nodes in Ireland.
- ClimbFinder's method is published (https://climbfinder.com/en/difficulty-points and https://climbfinder.com/en/climb-categories). Difficulty points follow the Encyclopedia Cotacol: the climb is cut into sections (100 m classically, "pieces of just 25 meters" now) and each section gets points that grow steeply with gradient, but the exact per-section formula is not published. Category thresholds in difficulty points: 5th >= 25, 4th >= 75, 3rd >= 150, 2nd >= 300, 1st >= 600, HC > 900, SHC >= 1500 ("no category" below 25; listing needs at least 5 points). ClimbFinder says Strava-style categorisation uses "a simple formula based on the average ascent and length".
- I fitted the per-section formula: points = sum over 100 m sections of (gradient in %)^2 / 10. On the GLO-30 profile of Wicklow Gap (last 6.8 km, `elevation.md`) this sums to 2,022 / 10 = 202.2, against ClimbFinder's published 202 for that climb. One climb fit, so it is a calibration, not a proof. A rough cross-check on three other Irish climbs (Priest's Leap from Coomhola Bridge 5.4 km at 8.2%, Knockgour 3.6 km at 10.6%, Mount Leinster 2.6 km at 13.3%) uses the numbers 436, 448 and 489 from ClimbFinder's Ireland ranking page, which I assume (not verified) are difficulty points; sum(g^2)/10 on the average gradient alone gives 363, 404 and 460, i.e. 7-17% low, which is the direction expected since real sections vary around the mean. That is consistent but not a proof.

## Derivation approach

Inputs: `data/osm/<region>.osm.pbf` (ODbL) and Copernicus GLO-30 tiles (`elevation.md`). Output: one record per directed climb: id, name, geometry, start/summit node, length_m, gain_m, avg_grad_pct, max_100m_grad_pct, points, category. Every threshold below is a number; the few judgement calls (which alternative to keep, tie-breaks) have a stated tie-break rule.

1. Road set. A way is a candidate iff `highway` is in {primary, secondary, tertiary, unclassified, residential, living_street} or the `*_link` of primary/secondary/tertiary, AND NOT (`access` in {no, private} unless `bicycle` in {yes, designated, permissive}) AND NOT `bicycle=no`. `highway=road`, `trunk`, `motorway`, `service`, `track`, `path`, `cycleway` are excluded; `area=yes` ways are skipped. One-way handling: `oneway=yes` means forward only and `oneway=-1` backward only, unless `oneway:bicycle=no` (then both); everything else is two-way. `junction=roundabout` ways are NOT skipped (see step 3).
2. Elevation per way. Sample GLO-30 bilinearly (as `scripts/elevation_profile.py`) every 25 m along the way geometry, plus at every node. For ways with `bridge=yes` or `tunnel=yes`, replace samples by linear interpolation between the elevations at the way's end nodes (a DSM reads the bridge deck or the ground above the tunnel). No-data cells: interpolate linearly across gaps of <= 100 m, otherwise split the chain there. (The median filter is applied later, once per chain; see step 3.)
3. Directed chains. Build directed chains by following, from each junction node or way end, the continuation at the next junction: the outgoing way with the same `ref` (else the same `name`); if none, the way whose bearing deviates least from the incoming bearing, provided the deviation is <= 60 degrees and its class rank differs by <= 1 from the current class (rank order as in step 1); otherwise the chain ends. Where several outgoing ways share the incoming `ref` (dual carriageways), prefer the one whose bearing differs least from the incoming bearing; on a tie take the lowest way id. Roundabouts are traversed: on entering a `junction=roundabout` way pick the exit with the smallest bearing change relative to the entry. Maximum chain length 25 km; when the cutoff is reached start a new chain 15 km before it, so the new chain overlaps the old one by 15 km and no climb shorter than 15 km is split (the duplicates are removed by step 8). Generate chains in both directions of every two-way road. Chain profile: first concatenate the per-way samples in travel order, resample the whole chain to a uniform 25 m grid by linear interpolation of elevation against distance, then apply the 3-sample median filter once on the chain profile (not per way). The candidate-ascent steps below run on this chain profile.
4. Candidate ascents along a chain profile (25 m samples). Find alternating turning points: a local minimum or maximum is confirmed once the profile moves H = 5 m away from it. Each minimum-to-maximum pair is a candidate ascent. Merge two consecutive ascents when the descent between them loses <= 10 m of elevation and is <= 400 m long; the merged climb starts at the first minimum and ends at the higher maximum.
5. Trim approaches. At the start of each candidate, drop leading 100 m steps while the following 300 m rises less than 2.5% on average (gain over the next 300 m below 7.5 m); apply the mirror rule at the end. Trimming never leaves a climb shorter than 600 m.
6. Acceptance thresholds, after trimming. Length >= 500 m AND gain (end elevation minus start elevation) >= 30 m AND average gradient >= 3.0% AND difficulty points >= 25 (ClimbFinder's category-5 floor). Reject otherwise.
7. Score. Cut the climb into 100 m sections from its start (the last, partial section is weighted by its length / 100). For each, g = section gradient in % clamped to [0, 25] (values above 25% are treated as DEM artefacts). points = sum over sections of g^2 / 10 x (section length / 100). Category by points: >= 1500 SHC, >= 900 HC, >= 600 cat 1, >= 300 cat 2, >= 150 cat 3, >= 75 cat 4, >= 25 cat 5 (ClimbFinder's published thresholds; the per-section formula is fitted, see Hands-on check). Also record the simple Strava-style product length_m x avg_grad_pct as a cross-check only. Report max_100m_grad as the maximum section gradient before clamping, from sections starting at multiples of 25 m with a 100 m window.
8. Summit and deduplicate. The summit of an accepted climb is the 25 m sample of maximum elevation (first one on ties), snapped to the nearest chain node within 25 m for naming and dedupe (if none is within 25 m the summit is the sample itself). Two candidates sharing the same summit node: process them in descending order of points and keep the first; keep each later one only if less than 50% of its length lies on edges already used by kept climbs (so "from Glendalough" and "from Knockalt"-style alternatives survive, near-identical ones do not).
9. Naming. Summit name in order: (a) nearest node with `mountain_pass=yes` and a `name` within 300 m of the summit; (b) nearest `natural=saddle` with a name within 300 m; (c) nearest named `natural=peak` within 500 m; (d) the `name` of the way covering most of the climb's length (>= 50%); (e) its `ref`; (f) nearest named `place=*` within 2 km; (g) "Climb at <lat>,<lon>" (4 decimals). Start name: nearest named `place` in {city, town, village, hamlet, locality} within 1,000 m of the start; the title is "<summit name> from <start name>", or just the summit name if there is no such place. (Where Wikidata has an item within 300 m of the summit, store its QID as a cross-reference; do not use it for naming, since OSM has the same names.)
10. Along-route matching (the feature the question asks for). A climb is "on" a route when >= 60% of the climb's length (by OSM way ids, same direction) lies on the route; "near" the route when its start node is within 500 m of the route line and the summit within 1,000 m.
11. Acceptance test of an implementation: on the Irish extract, recover >= 90% of ClimbFinder's published Ireland list that meet the step-6 thresholds, with points within +-20% of ClimbFinder's (ClimbFinder pages can be used for evaluation only; do not ship their data).

Dry run, hand-checkable: `uv run python scripts/climb_spec_dryrun.py data/samples/wicklow_gap_profile_glo30.csv` (reference implementation of steps 4-7 on one profile; constants are the ones above). Wicklow Gap from Laragh, GLO-30 profile of `elevation.md` (7,725 m, starts 151.3 m, ends 473.6 m):

- Median filter and turning points (H = 5 m): extrema at 100 m (153.9 m), 550 m (138.7 m), 7,725 m (473.6 m). The only ascent is 550 m to 7,725 m: 7,175 m long, 138.7 to 473.6 m, gain 334.9 m, average 4.67%, 204.9 points. The small bumps in the first kilometre (the highest, 143.4 m at 700 m, is only 4.7 m above the 138.7 m minimum) never reach the 5 m swing, so they do not split the climb.
- Trim: the 300 m window starting at 550, 650, 750, 850, 950, 1,050 and 1,150 m gains 1.9, 0.1, -2.5, 0.8, 5.4, 7.4 and 9.5 m. The first six are below the 7.5 m (2.5%) bar and are trimmed (1,050 m only just: 7.4 m, 2.47%); the window at 1,150 m passes, so the trimmed climb starts at 1,150 m. It runs 1,150 to 7,725 m: length 6,575 m, 141.5 to 473.6 m, gain 332.1 m, average 5.05%, 204.0 points, category 3. It passes every threshold (length >= 500, gain >= 30, average >= 3.0, points >= 25).
- The removed approach (550 to 1,150 m): length 600 m, 138.7 to 141.5 m, gain 2.8 m, average 0.47%, 1.0 points. It fails the gain (< 30 m), gradient (< 3.0%) and points (< 25) thresholds, so it is rejected as a standalone climb and trimmed off the real one. The flat-looking first 550 m is a descent from 153.9 m to 138.7 m (15.2 m), too large to merge (> 10 m), so the climb cannot start before 550 m.
- Naming: the OSM node "Wicklow Gap" (`mountain_pass=yes`, `ele=480`) is 93 m from the profile's last sample (the end of the OSM way, about 90 m before the crest), so rule (a) fires. The nearest named village to the start (1,150 m, 53.0118 N 6.3238 W) is Glendalough at 160 m (Laragh is 1,795 m away). Title: "Wicklow Gap from Glendalough", identical to ClimbFinder's.
- Comparison with ClimbFinder (6.8 km, 337 m, 4.9%, 202 points, category 3): spec result 6.58 km (-3.3%), 332.1 m (-1.5%), 5.05%, 204.0 points (+1.0%), category 3. The same run on the SRTM-derived profile trims to a start at 950 m: 6,775 m, 335.5 m, 4.95%, 220.3 points, category 3 (the noisier DEM inflates points by 9%; category unchanged). The SRTM run also produces a spurious 100 m ascent at 0-100 m (5.7 m gain) which fails length (< 500 m), gain and points and is rejected.
- Naive baseline: the whole 7,725 m track from its start without trimming scores 322.3 m gain at 4.17%, 205.2 points; the spec's trimming and merging corrects the length by 1,150 m and the gradient from 4.17% to 5.05%, which is what separates a "climb" from "the road from where I happened to start".

## Verdict

No open climb dataset exists; derive from OSM + Copernicus GLO-30 using the spec above. Every curated climb database is proprietary or unlicensed (ClimbFinder, Quaeldich, climbbybike, cols-cyclisme, Cycling Cols: all rights reserved, with ClimbFinder and Quaeldich forbidding copying in their terms) and Strava segments are excluded by the API Agreement. Open data supplies only names and points: Wikidata (CC0) has 22 Irish passes (21 with coordinates, 8 with elevation), OSM (ODbL) has 23 pass and 17 saddle nodes. The derivation is feasible and, on the single climb tested, reproduces ClimbFinder to within 1.5% on gain, 3% on length and 1% on difficulty points, with the same category (3) and the same name. ClimbFinder's per-section formula is not published; the sum(g^2)/10 over 100 m sections fits one data point, and three other Irish climbs are consistent in direction but only on assumed numbers, so the point scale needs calibrating on more climbs before categories are trusted. Naming from OSM pass nodes alone would cover only about a tenth of the 239 climbs ClimbFinder lists for Ireland; the fallbacks in step 9 are needed.

## Open questions for Eric

- Is a +-20% points tolerance enough, or should we calibrate the per-section formula on a larger set of climbs (needs hand-copied ClimbFinder numbers for evaluation only)?
- Cycling Cols invites "data use or partnerships" by email. Worth asking about a licence for the 49 Irish cols, or is derived data enough?
- Should unpaved or `highway=track` climbs (gravel) be in scope? The spec excludes them.

- Step 1 excludes `highway=trunk` (Irish N-roads) as climb candidates. Several ClimbFinder Irish climbs run on N-roads, so this may cost recall in the step-11 test. Include trunk (with a quiet-road preference handled elsewhere), or keep it out?
