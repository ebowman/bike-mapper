# Non-ride stop priors for Ireland: open data and literature

## Question

The planner scores a route by summing per-junction stop probabilities over seven OSM-derived
event classes (signal, xing_signal, stop, give_way, yield_infer, yield_equal, rb_entry). Today
those probabilities come from one rider's GPS matched to OSM (`data/model/stop_model.json`,
pooled Ireland+Berlin, Berlin-weighted; the two regions disagree on give_way by 7-10x). Can any
open, non-ride source for Ireland supply per-class stop probabilities or signal red time, so the
table can default to a *structural prior* and Eric's rides become a sanity check rather than the
source? Checked hands-on on 2026-10-21: Smart Dublin / See.Sense, DCC/DLR/SDCC SCATS, NTA
GTFS-Realtime, Strava Metro, and the literature.

**1. See.Sense / Smart Dublin.** Not published. The 2019 Smart Docklands pilot (200 riders, SynchroniCity) produced near-miss, surface-quality, route and speed aggregates for the Council only; nothing on either portal, and nothing per segment or junction. The only cycling datasets are counters (hourly counts at fixed sites: DCC `dublin-city-centre-cycle-counts`, Fingal `cycle-counter-*`, DLR `bicycle-traffic-counts-dlr`, the `pedestrian-and-cycle-counter-api-for-dublin-region`), bike-share feeds and infrastructure polylines. None carries a stop, dwell, braking or speed-drop field.

**2. SCATS.** What is open is detector *volume*, not timing: DCC and DLR publish hourly `Sum_Volume` per inductive-loop detector (DCC March 2025: 1,001 sites, 16,453 detectors, 24 hourly stamps per day; the `Weighted_*` columns are empty), SDCC publishes 15-minute `flow`, `cong` and `dsat` (degree of saturation, 0-200 %; 43 % of rows are 0, 95th percentile about 90 %) for 18 sites in 2018-2019. No file has cycle length, phase split, offset or green time, so **a per-approach red fraction cannot be derived**. The site lists are the useful part: DCC types 850 sites as Junction (502 incl. Luas) vs Pedestrian/Pelican (348), SDCC types 375 as Junction (162) vs Pedestrian Crossing (213). That is the open fact that matters for the model: in Dublin roughly 45 % of signal sites are standalone pedestrian crossings, which rest on green for traffic and stop a cyclist only when a pedestrian has pressed the button, whereas SCATS junctions cycle continuously. Dublin cycle lengths from press: minimum 60 s, maximum 80 s (120 s pre-2020), SCATS re-plans every 2 minutes.

**3. GTFS-Realtime.** Open under CC-BY 4.0 with a fair-usage policy, but a subscription key is required (not registered here; the unauthenticated feed returns 401) and the refresh interval is not documented anywhere on the portal. Assessment: bus dwell away from stops could in principle reveal red phases, but only on bus corridors, the vehicle-position cadence (minutes) is coarser than a Dublin red phase (20-50 s), and SCATS gives buses green extension/priority plus most Dublin stops sit within 30 m of a junction, so dwell cannot be separated into stop, queue and red. Not worth building.

**4. Strava Metro.** Eligibility is "organizations that plan, own, or maintain active transportation" infrastructure, with an approved Project Type; individuals are only Authorized Users of such a licensee; consultants need a written contract with one; academic access is non-commercial. Licensees "shall not publicly distribute any Strava Data", may not build "competitive products" and may not use the data to "train, fine-tune, evaluate, benchmark, or validate any machine-learning model". A personal or consumer route planner does not qualify, and even a licensed planner could not ship edge-level speeds or stop rates. Not usable.

**5. Literature numbers.**

(a) *Signals.* With uniform (random) arrivals the fraction of cyclists stopped is the red share, p = 1 - g/C (the uniform term of the HCM bicycle delay formula in FHWA-RD-98-108, d = 0.5 C (1 - g/C)^2 / (1 - (g/C) min(V/c,1)), gives the same expectation: average wait red/2 times p). ECF's worked example: 12 s green in a 60 s cycle gives p = 0.80 and 24 s average wait, 19.2 s expected delay. Ashford (2014) measured exactly this with a Garmin Edge 500 over 80 Christchurch peak trips: stopped at **44 % of signals on the major-arterial route where the available green averaged 56 % of the cycle**, and at **65 % on the minor-arterial route where green averaged 34 %** (uniform model predicts 44 % and 66 %); overall 54 % of signals, 33 s per stop, 18 s per intersection, 15 % of trip time; inner-city 250 m spacing gave 40 % / 60 %. Compliance discount for Ireland: RSA 2015, 1 in 8 cyclists ran reds nationally (60 sites, 26,126 cyclists); Richardson & Caulfield 2015, Dublin city centre, 61.9 % of 3,064 ran reds overall but only **18.6 % on cycle lanes (carriageway)** versus 97.8 % on segregated tracks, about one in four violations on the motor phase. ECF/CROW benchmarks for context: main cycle routes should stay under 1 stop/km and 20 s/km (CROW 1993: 0.5 stops/km, 15 s/km; LTN 1/20 LOS2: 2 stops/km), average waits at signals should be 15 s (good) to 20 s (moderate), maximum 90-100 s.

(b) *Yield-controlled junctions.* No cyclist stop-rate table exists, so the structural model is gap acceptance: a cyclist arriving at a yield line is forced to stop when the lag to the next conflicting vehicle is shorter than the critical gap. For Poisson conflicting flow q (veh/s) and critical gap t_c, p(forced stop) = 1 - exp(-q t_c). Cyclist accepted gaps are about 4.5 s (4.52 s, Beijing, versus 5.79 s for pedestrians; HCM pedestrian critical gap 4.4 s). With t_c = 5 s: q = 20 veh/h gives 0.027; 40 veh/h 0.054; 100 veh/h 0.13; 400 veh/h 0.43. Daytime hourly flow is about 7 % of AADT, and in Ireland a left turn onto the major road conflicts with one direction only, so a regional road of 3,000 AADT presents q of about 100 veh/h to a minor-road cyclist, a tertiary/unclassified road of 1,000-1,500 AADT about 40, and a local road about 20. Stop-sign compliance (Silva 2015, Seattle: 19 % full stop, 55 % rolling, 25 % no stop; Minneapolis 58 % "stopped" at 411 observations; 66 % in Woodside CA unattended) says that absent traffic most cyclists roll, so a stop sign adds little over a give-way unless sight lines force a near-stop, which is precisely where Irish practice places STOP rather than YIELD.

(c) *Irish/UK cyclist junction delay.* No measured Irish or UK stop-probability or delay-per-junction study was found; the Irish evidence is the two compliance studies above, DCC's radar cyclist-detection trials (13 junctions by 2019, 40 funded) and the 2018 Department of Transport note that Dublin inter-greens follow UK TAL 1/06 assumptions. The UK LTN 1/20 level-of-service thresholds (2-4 stops/km) are design targets, not measurements.

Ratios to the signal class, which is what the `weight_300` scheme and `flow.brf` actually consume: prior signal : give_way : yield_infer : yield_equal : stop : xing : rb = 1 : 0.30 : 0.15 : 0.075 : 0.375 : 0.15 : 0.375; combined (unrounded CSV p) 1 : 0.259 : 0.102 : 0.133 : 0.132 : (1.0 assigned, 0.120 raw) : 0; Ireland (unrounded CSV p) 1 : 0.414 : 0.161 : 0.490 : 0.151 : 0.461 : 0. The prior reproduces the combined give_way and yield_infer ratios within the empirical confidence intervals without using a single ride, and it agrees with the *Ireland* data (not the model file) that a standalone crossing stops a cyclist far less often than a junction signal. It disagrees in direction on stop signs (prior above give_way, Eric below it) and gives roundabouts a non-zero value where Eric has 22 passes and no stops. The absolute level is 2.5-4x Eric's: the gap model counts every forced interruption, Eric's instruments count full stops of several seconds after a 20-30 m rolling approach, and Eric's Irish signals are mostly suburban/rural actuated sites rather than the SCATS grid; Ashford's 44-65 % with the same Garmin instrument shows the gap between the two is route mix, not the instrument.

## Verdict

**Default to `prior`.** No open Irish dataset gives per-class stop probabilities or red time: See.Sense
was never published, SCATS exports are volumes without timing, GTFS-R needs a key and cannot isolate
red phases, Strava Metro is closed to individuals and products. The literature does give a defensible
structural table: red share for signals (within 2 points of Ashford's Garmin measurements) and a
gap-acceptance model for every yield class, tied to conflicting flow by road class. Its ratios match
Eric's combined data on the powered classes (give_way, yield_infer), fix the signal/crossing split and
the zero roundabout weight, and generalise to other riders and to streets Eric does not ride. `ireland`
is the check set (xing/signal 0.461 and give_way 0.414 lean the prior's way) but rests on 42-79 passes
for three classes; `combined` is Berlin-weighted on exactly the disputed classes. Flag `stop`: Eric
rolls stop signs (1.6-2.1 %). The 2.5-4x absolute gap only affects `flow_scale`, a per-request knob.

Switching weight sets: set `FLOW_WEIGHTS=combined|ireland|prior` in the environment, or call `flowplan.stop_model.load(weights=...)`; the default is `prior`.
